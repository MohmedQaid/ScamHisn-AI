## Peer-reviewed and technical

IEEE-published items are [45]–[49]. "Verified" states how the record was confirmed. Where the abstract could not be opened, the relevance note is limited to what the title supports and says so.

[45] M. Khonji, Y. Iraqi, and A. Jones, "Phishing detection: A literature survey," IEEE Commun. Surveys Tuts., vol. 15, no. 4, pp. 2091–2121, 2013, doi: 10.1109/SURV.2013.032213.00009. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1109/SURV.2013.032213.00009 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract read via Semantic Scholar. Relevance: surveys phishing detection and argues there is no single solution, so several techniques are combined — support for a layered rule-plus-model design.

[46] S. Marchal, J. François, R. State, and T. Engel, "PhishStorm: Detecting phishing with streaming analytics," IEEE Trans. Netw. Service Manag., vol. 11, no. 4, pp. 458–471, Dec. 2014, doi: 10.1109/TNSM.2014.2377295. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSM.2014.2377295 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata (resolves to IEEE Xplore document 6975177; Xplore page body could not be read by script). Relevance (title-level only, abstract not opened): phishing detection framed as a real-time streaming problem.

[47] A. C. Bahnsen, E. C. Bohorquez, S. Villegas, J. Vargas, and F. A. González, "Classifying phishing URLs using recurrent neural networks," in Proc. 2017 APWG Symp. Electronic Crime Research (eCrime), Apr. 2017, pp. 1–8, doi: 10.1109/ECRIME.2017.7945048. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1109/ECRIME.2017.7945048 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata (resolves to IEEE Xplore document 7945048; page body not readable by script). Relevance (title-level only, abstract not opened): classifying phishing from the URL string with a recurrent neural network.

[48] F. Heiding, B. Schneier, A. Vishwanath, J. Bernstein, and P. S. Park, "Devising and detecting phishing emails using large language models," IEEE Access, vol. 12, pp. 42131–42146, 2024, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3375882. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3375882 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract read via Semantic Scholar. Relevance: compares phishing emails written by GPT-4 with manually designed ones, i.e. evidence that LLMs lower the cost of convincing phishing.

[49] T. Koide, H. Nakano, and D. Chiba, "ChatPhishDetector: Detecting phishing sites using large language models," IEEE Access, vol. 12, pp. 154381–154400, 2024, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3483905. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3483905 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract read (IEEE Access record via Semantic Scholar, and the arXiv preprint arXiv:2306.05816). Relevance: crawls a site, builds a prompt and lets an LLM judge it; reports that this identifies impersonated brands and social-engineering cues, including on multilingual sites, without training a classifier.

[50] K. Greshake, S. Abdelnabi, S. Mishra, C. Endres, T. Holz, and M. Fritz, "Not what you've signed up for: Compromising real-world LLM-integrated applications with indirect prompt injection," in Proc. 16th ACM Workshop Artif. Intell. Secur. (AISec '23), Nov. 2023, pp. 79–90, doi: 10.1145/3605764.3623985. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1145/3605764.3623985 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract read on arXiv (arXiv:2302.12173). Relevance: shows that text an LLM application retrieves (a web page, a message) can carry instructions that override the application's own prompt — the reason scanned page content must be treated as data.

[51] P. Kintis et al., "Hiding in plain sight: A longitudinal study of combosquatting abuse," in Proc. 2017 ACM SIGSAC Conf. Comput. Commun. Secur. (CCS '17), Oct. 2017, pp. 569–586, doi: 10.1145/3133956.3134002. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3134002 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract read via Semantic Scholar. Relevance: first large-scale study of domains that combine a trademark with extra words (brand-plus-keyword impersonation), using more than 468 billion DNS records over almost six years.

[52] P. Agten, W. Joosen, F. Piessens, and N. Nikiforakis, "Seven months' worth of mistakes: A longitudinal study of typosquatting abuse," in Proc. Netw. Distrib. Syst. Secur. Symp. (NDSS), 2015, doi: 10.14722/ndss.2015.23058. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.14722/ndss.2015.23058 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026). Verified: doi.org metadata; abstract not opened (only a machine-generated summary on Semantic Scholar was seen). Relevance (title-level): longitudinal measurement of typosquatting of popular domains.

[53] S. Hao, A. Kantchelian, B. Miller, V. Paxson, and N. Feamster, "PREDATOR: Proactive recognition and elimination of domain abuse at time-of-registration," in Proc. 2016 ACM SIGSAC Conf. Comput. [55] O. K. Sahingoz, E. Buber, O. Demir, and B. Diri, "Machine learning based phishing detection from URLs," Expert Syst. Appl., vol. 117, pp. 345–357, Mar. 2019, doi: 10.1016/j.eswa.2018.09.029. Intell., vol. 104, Art. no. 104347, Sep. 2021, doi: 10.1016/j.engappai.2021.104347. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engappai.2021.104347 (accessed 24 Nov. 2026).
