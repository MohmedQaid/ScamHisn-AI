# Fact sheet

Retrieved 24 Nov 2026. Reference numbers point to `docs/research/sources.md`. Every value below was read on the cited page or in the cited file during this session; nothing is from memory.

"Primary" = the publisher of the figure (government portal, police site, the organisation that ran the survey, the vendor's own pricing page). "Secondary" = a news outlet or agency reporting someone else's figure. WAM is the state news agency: it is marked secondary, but it is the closest available record of official statements.

Confidence: high = primary source, or an official statement carried consistently by two or more outlets; medium = one secondary source, or a survey / self-reported figure; low = old, loosely defined, or internally inconsistent.

Answer for someone in Abu Dhabi: the Aman service of Abu Dhabi Police (8002626, SMS 2828, aman@adpolice.gov.ae, ADPolice app, https://aman.adpolice.gov.ae). That is what Abu Dhabi Police name in every fraud warning read ([19], [32], [33], [35]). "eCrime" is the Dubai Police platform and is the wrong label for an Abu Dhabi user.

One caveat the app should respect: the official Aman FAQ says Aman takes information, not formal complaints — in its words, «أمان تعتبر قناة أمنية تستقبل المعلومات الأمنية وليس قناة فتح بلاغات» [13]. A victim who has lost money and needs a case opened should also file a report with the police (police station, or the "Police Station in Your Phone" service that police statements list next to Aman [19], [33]) and tell their bank. So the accurate wording is "report it to Aman", not "file a case through Aman".

## 5. Conflicts between sources

- Campaign name. Gulf News' Dh140 million story calls it "Be Careful" [30], as does The National [31]; WAM and a second Gulf News story call it "Be Cautious" and say it is the sixth edition [18], [32]. Use "Be Cautious", the WAM wording.
- Campaign length. WAM and Gulf News say three months [18], [32]; The National quotes a police director saying it lasts until the end of the year [31]. Launched in late October 2025, these give different end dates.
- Launch date. Gulf News and The National are dated 22 Oct 2025 [30], [31], [32]; the WAM item is datelined 23 Oct 2025 [18].
- What the 15,642 counts. Gulf News describes them as cybercrime cases [30]; The National quotes the officer describing them as online fraud cases [31].
- Daily cyberattacks. More than 800,000 a day over recent months (Council, May 2026, per Gulf News) [36]; 640,000 in one day (chairman, Sep 2026) [37]; nearly 750,000 attempts thwarted over recent months — a total, not a daily rate (Oct 2026) [38]. These are different measures and should not be merged into one number. If one is used, say which.
- Share of people scammed. GASA: 1 in 3 lost money in the last year, and more than half experienced a scam in the past 12 months [24]. Visa: 46% experienced a financial scam in the past 12 months [27]; 49% have ever fallen for a scam [28]. GASA 2024: 27% lost money [26]. Different questions, samples and years; cite each with its own wording.
- TAMM users. The same Gulf News article gives 4.5 million registered users in one paragraph and 4.6 million users in another [42].
- Gemini Search grounding on the free tier. The per-model tables for Gemini 3.x mark it as not available on the free tier, while the tools table on the same page lists 500 requests per day free without restricting that to 2.5 models [61]. Treat free-tier grounding on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` as unavailable until tested.
- Aman as a "reporting" channel. Police press statements tell the public to report fraud through Aman [19], [33]; the Aman FAQ says Aman receives information and is not a channel for opening a formal report [13]. Both are true; see section 2.
